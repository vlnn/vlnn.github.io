There are several methods that can be applied to understanding the task:

* [Polya's method](polya-s-method.md)*:* ask what is the unknown, what is the data, what is the condition connecting them? 
> [!note]
> It reminds the data you need for [writing the test](test-driven-development.md) — what behavior we are looking for, in which context, what would connect them. (even easier we usually know what behavior we are looking for, it's not the unknown!).
* Analogy
* Inversion
* Abstraction and generalization
* Constraint identification
* [Feynman technique](feynman-technique.md)

One of main biases for me is even when I think I understand the task I really don't. Usually tasks are represented on [wrong level of abstraction](wrong-level-of-abstraction.md): either too specific (and instead of solving the whole family of similar problems you will be solving one exact thing that was brought to the attention) or too general (and you resolving the problem you think you should to solve, not the problem the stakeholder has).
