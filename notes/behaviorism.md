Behaviorism was a prominent theory of psychology in the early twentieth century, intended to make human [behavior](behavior.md) more susceptible to quantitative experiment. Its philosophy is that psychology should be understood in terms of what’s observable: current/historical environmental stimuli and their consequent [behaviors](behavior.md). Learning is assumed to be an automatic process, shaping behavior in response to associations between events and their consequences.

In short, [behaviorism](behaviorism.md) uses [positivist methodology](positivist-methodology.md)  to describe [operant conditioning](operant-conditioning.md), in which [behaviors](behavior.md) reinforced by their consequences (environmental [feedback](feedback.md)) become more frequent — the [behavioral evolution](behavioral-evolution-b0e26f96.md) that is progressing within a lifetime, not across generations.
