My idea is to effectively consume a complex information with sequence of depth-graded overviews of the sources.

Imagine Quantum Physics course you're interested in. Traditionally you would search for a good textbook, where good means "accessible enough" and "precise enough" at the same time. It's a hard problem, easily leading to yak shaving or canceling the project.

LLMs hit twice in that area:
* they generate complex long stuff (imagine vibecoded project)
* they simplify long stuff into short stuff quickly ("ELI5 the document" prompt works really good with modern models)

So practically the method becomes "use LLMs to generate a sequence of overviews with raising complexity for the learner's sequential understanding of the whole source". I can imagine how this could be used for code reviews, general texts, even music sheets.

This is similar to Bruner's spiral curriculum, but customized to the field of interest. It also reminds of Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, but with textual aid instead of teacher figure.
