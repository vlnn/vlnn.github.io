Intense, structured interval revision learning technique popularized in South Korea's highly competitive examination preparation culture (such as civil service, judicial, and bar exams).

Material is read several times, each time for a specific reason and in specific schedule relative to the exam date. Each full pass through the material takes half as long as the previous one. If the first read-through takes 8 units of time (weeks, days, whatever scale fits), the second takes 4, the third 2, the fourth 1. Four passes in 15 units total, versus 32 if you re-read at the original pace.

* **8** (Pass 1): ***Slow understanding***: read everything, work through examples, look things up. Confusion is expected here.
* **4** (Pass 2): ***Reinforcement***: read everything again but faster, since you already know the shape. Feedback on hard things starts here.
* **2** (Pass 3): ***Selection***: skim what you know, slow down only on the marked parts. Begin folding in problems and past-exam questions.
* **1** (Pass 4): ***Recall***: mostly checking that you can reproduce the content from the headings and marks alone.

It is inverse of [space repetition method](method-of-spaced-repetition.md), as instead of expanding review periods, it smacks it together, providing the peak [memorization](memory-and-imagination-are-but-two-words-for-the-same-thing.md) at known date of the exam and not worrying much about keeping it for long.
